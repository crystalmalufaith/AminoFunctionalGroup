1. Compound & Structure

Compound: Glycine (Amino acid containing an amino group -NH₂)

Chemical Formula: C₂H₅NO₂

Condensed Structure: NH₂–CH₂–COOH

This simple amino acid contains both amino and carboxyl groups attached to the same carbon atom.

2. Isomer

Isomer: Methyl carbamate (H₃C–O–C(=O)–NH₂)

While glycine has separate amino and carboxyl groups, methyl carbamate contains a carbamate linkage (-O–C(=O)–NH₂), altering its chemical properties.

3. Polymer — Polyglycine

Repeating unit: –NH–CH₂–CO–

Formed through condensation of glycine molecules, polyglycine exhibits flexibility and protein-like structure.

Properties: Biodegradable, flexible, and can form stable peptide chains.

4. Reaction — Peptide Bond Formation

Reaction Type: Condensation

NH₂–CH₂–COOH + NH₂–CH₂–COOH → NH₂–CH₂–CO–NH–CH₂–COOH + H₂O

This peptide bond formation links amino acids, releasing water and forming dipeptides—fundamental in protein synthesis.

5. Preparation / Synthesis

Industrial: ClCH₂COOH + NH₃ → NH₂CH₂COOH + HCl (amination of chloroacetic acid)

Laboratory: Strecker synthesis (Aldehyde + NH₃ + HCN → aminonitrile → hydrolysis → amino acid)

Natural: Produced enzymatically from serine via serine hydroxymethyltransferase.
